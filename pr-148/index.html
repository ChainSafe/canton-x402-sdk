Non-custodial

Payers prepare and sign on their own participant. The facilitator only executes a transaction the payer already signed — it never sees a private key, and never picks the payer's holdings.

Why it is non-custodial

Canton-native

Settlement is a Splice Token Standard transfer through the instrument's TransferFactory, delivered straight to your party through its TransferPreapproval — one atomic Canton transaction.

What settlement does

Standards-aligned

The x402 v2 wire format and the Canton exact scheme — the quote in the 402's PAYMENT-REQUIRED header, the signed payment in PAYMENT-SIGNATURE, verify and settle at the facilitator.

The protocol

x402 makes an ordinary HTTP request payable: a client that calls a paid endpoint gets back 402 Payment Required, signs a payment, and retries with it. The facilitator is the HTTP service that turns that signed payment into a real, settled payment on the Canton Network — so your server never has to speak to a ledger, hold a wallet, or run a node. The facilitator explains what it does and why it is non-custodial.

Payment flow ​

A paid request is an ordinary HTTP request, retried once. The first try comes back 402 Payment Required with the price and terms. The payer prepares a Canton transfer for exactly that amount, signs it with its own key, and retries with the signed payment attached. Your server hands it to the facilitator, which checks that the signed transfer pays exactly your terms, then executes it on Canton, where the funds land at your party. Custody stays with the payer throughout: the facilitator never sees a private key, and can only submit the transaction the payer signed.
