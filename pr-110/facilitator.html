The facilitator ​

This page zooms in on steps 5 and 6 of the payment flow, the facilitator's half. Before any money moves, verify enforces a handful of guarantees — your payment can't be replayed, tampered with, or under-paid — and settle re-runs all of them before executing on Canton. The facilitator never moves a payer's funds on its own authority: the only write that moves them is executeAndWaitForTransaction of a payer-signed transaction.

What verify guarantees ​

These run in order, with no chain write. The first six are shared code — the same @chainsafe/x402-core verifier the SDK exposes — so a payer can pre-flight its payload against them, and they refuse with the Canton scheme spec's codes, keeping the code they used before in extensions.reason. The last is the facilitator's alone, since only it holds the state involved:

  1. Right scheme and network — the payload, the requirements, and the network this facilitator is bound to all agree (unsupported_scheme or invalid_network otherwise).
  2. The transaction decodes — preparedTransaction is one gzip member within the size caps, its node graph is well-formed, and it holds the one transfer. Its sender is the payer (the payload names none), and checks 3 to 6 report it. A payload missing its proof is invalid_exact_canton_missing_proof, and one that does not decode is invalid_exact_canton_malformed_payload.
  3. Still executable — the signed transfer's own executeBefore is far enough in the future to settle (invalid_exact_canton_expired). The quote itself no longer expires; the deadline that counts is inside what the payer signed.
  4. The key belongs to the payer — the fingerprint derived from the payload's publicKey must match the :: suffix of the transfer's sender, so a caller can't sign as a party it doesn't control (invalid_exact_canton_signature_invalid otherwise).
  5. The signature is valid — the hash recomputed from the transaction itself must equal preparedTxHash, and the payer's key must have signed it (invalid_exact_canton_signature_invalid otherwise).
  6. The signed transfer actually pays you. This is what binds the payment to your terms: the transfer's receiver, instrument and amount are checked against the requirements, so a valid signature over a divergent transfer is rejected: invalid_exact_canton_merchant_mismatch, invalid_exact_canton_instrument_id_mismatch, invalid_exact_canton_amount_mismatch or invalid_exact_canton_memo_mismatch. The amount must be EQUAL, not merely sufficient — exact is the scheme's distinguishing property, and a payer who overpays has no refund path. The transfer's x402.memo must equal your extra.memo exactly — in both directions, so a transfer carrying a memo you did not quote is refused as well.
  7. No replay, and you're an allowed destination — the payment has not settled before (the ledger is the real guard: a replayed transfer names input holdings that are already spent), and for an authenticated merchant payTo must be a party that key is allowed to receive at (your destination policy). A destination denial comes back as transfer_mismatch with a destination_not_allowed detail on verify, and as unauthorized on settle.

What settlement does ​

Funds are real Splice Amulet (Canton Coin), transferred through the Token Standard's TransferFactory in one atomic Canton transaction. When your party has a TransferPreapproval, that transfer is direct, so nothing is escrowed or locked up; without one, the Token Standard makes it an offer that completes only once you accept it. What is recorded is the signed transfer's amount, not the quoted floor, so an overpayment is not understated.
