The payment flow ​

A paid request end to end under exact on Canton — from the 402 your route returns to the on-ledger update id it settles with. This is what runs underneath the route you gated in Getting started, and The facilitator then zooms in on steps 5 and 6. You write almost none of it: the merchant side (steps 1, 5 and 6) is handled via @chainsafe/x402-express, and the payer side (steps 2–4) via the payer's own SDK, @chainsafe/x402-server-sdk. You configure two things yourself: your route's price and terms in the x402-express middleware (step 1), and the max header size on your resource server (step 4).

  1. Client hits a gated route (402)

    Your route answers 402 Payment Required with the spec's PaymentRequired, { x402Version: 2, resource, accepts: [...] }, base64-encoded in the PAYMENT-REQUIRED header and as the body. resource names what is being paid for: the request URL and the route's description. Each accepts entry carries scheme, network, amount, asset, extra.assetTransferMethod, extra.instrumentId, extra.executeBeforeSeconds (120 by default, and optionally an extra.memo), payTo (your Canton party), resource and a maxTimeoutSeconds (60 by default).

  2. Payer builds the transfer

    The payer queries its Amulet holdings and fetches a TransferFactory from the Splice Token Standard registry for the asset — the validator scan-proxy for Amulet.

  3. Prepare and sign

    The payer calls Canton's interactive-submission/prepare to get the transaction blob and its hash, then Ed25519-signs that hash with its party key. Nothing is executed, and the private key never leaves the payer.

  4. Retry with X-PAYMENT

    The payment — the requirements it was built against as accepted (the accepts[] entry the payer chose), and the payload: assetTransferMethod, preparedTransaction, preparedTxHash, signature, publicKey, hashingSchemeVersion — is JSON-encoded, base64'd into an X-PAYMENT header, and the request is retried. That header carries the prepared transaction, gzipped, with its disclosed contracts.

  5. Merchant verifies, then settles

    Your middleware re-checks the payment against your own terms — scheme, network, payTo, resource, asset, maxTimeoutSeconds, and that it is neither underpriced nor expired — using the exact requirements the payment was built against, its accepted, which the facilitator then holds the signed transfer to, so a payment built for other terms can't pass. It then POSTs { x402Version, paymentPayload, paymentRequirements } to /v2/verify and, with settle: true (the default is false: verify only), to /v2/settle, with your API key. A payment that fails your terms or the facilitator's verify gets a fresh challenge (a new 402) instead of being settled.

  6. Facilitator executes on Canton

    /v2/settle re-runs every verify check, then calls Canton's executeAndWaitForTransaction with the payer-signed blob. On success it returns the update id as transaction, which the middleware puts on your response as X-PAYMENT-RESPONSE, and your handler runs with the verified payer on req.x402.

Raise your server's max header size

The X-PAYMENT header from step 4 runs to about 10 KB for a typical transfer — much of Node's ~16 KB default, which covers all of a request's headers together — so a Node/Express merchant server should set maxHeaderSize (e.g. --max-http-header-size) or the paid retry dies with 431 Request Header Fields Too Large before your handler ever runs. Step 6 of Getting started shows where it goes.
